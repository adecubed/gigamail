What's new

Every release, in plain words.

GigaMail went public on 15 August 2026. Since then, 28 releases. Here is what each one brought, grouped by version: the highlights first, then every release with its date.

0.6Your agent moves into the app

20 October – 4 November 2026 · 8 releases · latest: 0.6.7

  • Ask the agent from the console. The agent bar runs Claude Code, Codex or Google Antigravity inside GigaMail, shows what you are about to approve, remembers the conversation and can be stopped.
  • ChatGPT and Codex in one click, and Codex works without installing its CLI.
  • “Waiting for you” has its own icon with counters. Approving means sending, from every channel, and a request approved but never sent is picked up again.
  • A new look: dark theme, a blue option for those who prefer it, line icons, a new logo.
  • Spam filters that block a kind of mail, not only a sender; marking SPAM blocks the sender.
  • The agent reads what you see, and nothing hidden. Invisible text in a mail is left out and flagged; executable attachments never reach the agent.
  • A desktop app for Linux, as a Debian/Ubuntu package and an AppImage.

0.4An archive of its own, a lighter core

30 September – 6 October 2026 · 2 releases

  • Every mail, in full, forever. GigaMail keeps its own archive with attachments and full-text search, synced continuously, and imports Outlook’s history once. Mail that the server already deleted is still found.
  • The core does not carry a trade. What was specific to one business became optional extensions; appointments are off unless you switch them on.
  • What you approve is what goes out: same recipients, same account, the same attachment byte for byte.
  • A Claude Code plugin, a code-signed Windows installer, and approvals on Linux with a local PIN.
  • After an appointment GigaMail asks how it went and drafts a follow-up seven days later.

0.3Console, calendar and Google

4 – 15 September 2026 · 5 releases

  • A guided first launch, IMAP accounts checked before they are saved, and an installer that ships with each release and updates itself.
  • Mail and calendar talk to each other. The agent sees the calendar before proposing a time, and the time a client picks goes into the calendar, with a Zoom link if it is a video call.

  • Codex as a first-class agent, and a Codex plugin.
  • A guard against mails that give orders to the assistant, and moving a mail now asks for approval.

0.2Rules, and approvals from your phone

26 August – 1 September 2026 · 4 releases

0.1The gate

15 – 19 August 2026 · 5 releases

  • An MCP server for your agent, over stdio with no network port: 24 tools for Microsoft 365 and any IMAP/SMTP mailbox, plus the calendar.
  • Nothing destructive on the agent’s word alone. Sending, replying and deleting return a preview; a person approves, then the exact arguments shown are executed. Every write goes to an append-only log.
  • Approval moved out of the agent’s reach (0.1.1), one approval can no longer run twice (0.1.2), and approving needs the person at the machine, with Windows Hello or Touch ID (0.1.4).
  • On PyPI as gigamail, and verified with OpenClaw and Hermes.
