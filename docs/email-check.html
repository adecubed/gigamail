What this check can get wrong

It follows rules, it does not know the sender. Read its result as a list of things to look at, not as a verdict.

False alarms

  • Unknown senders and small companies. A real business whose domain looks like a brand (an apple- or poste- something) can be flagged as a look-alike.
  • Mail sent through a service. Newsletters and invoices often go out through Mailchimp, SendGrid or similar: Reply-To and the bounce address point elsewhere, and that is normal.
  • Forwarded mail. Forwarding often breaks SPF and sometimes DKIM, so an honest message can show failed sender checks.
  • Mistakes in the public lists. The lists of bad sites are kept by volunteers and can wrongly include an honest site; a brand's new domain can also look like an imitation.
  • Normal hidden text and forms. Newsletters hide a preview line; a real company's HTML attachment can contain a form.

Things it misses

  • New scam sites. Senders and links are looked up in public lists of known phishing and malware sites, updated every day, but a site set up this morning is not on them yet. It does not open links.
  • Sites that are not popular. Look-alikes are spotted for the 10,000 most visited sites and the most imitated brands, not for a small local bank or your supplier.
  • Text inside images. A mail that is only a picture of a message is not read.
  • Some files. It does not open .rar or .7z archives, and a PDF can hide code in compressed parts it does not unpack.
  • What you did not paste. Text copied from the screen has no headers, no attachments and no hidden part: open the saved .eml file for the full check.

When in doubt, contact the sender through a channel you already know, not through the mail itself. This page is a free helper, not a security product, and comes with no guarantee.

Questions

How to spot a phishing email?

To tell if an email is real or a scam, check who it is really from (the address, not the name), where replies go, and whether the links open the address they show. Be wary of pressure: a deadline, a locked account, a request for a password or a payment. This checker looks at all of that for you, and at the sender checks (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) when you paste the original message.

What is hidden text in an email?

Text that is in the message but not on your screen: white on white, a font size of zero, moved off the page, or inside an HTML comment. Spammers use it to fool filters. Now it is also used to give instructions to AI agents that read your mail, which see all of it.

Does it check attachments?

Yes. Drop a single file (a PDF, a Word or Excel file, a zip) to check just that file, or open the saved mail (.eml) to check all its attachments together with the mail. It looks at what each file really is, not what its name says: programs disguised as documents, double extensions like invoice.pdf.exe, Office files with macros or that load a template from the internet, PDFs with JavaScript, web pages that ask for a password, and archives that hide programs or are locked with a password. Files are read in your browser and never opened.

Can an AI agent be tricked by an email?

Yes. An agent reads the whole message, hidden parts included, and may follow instructions written there, such as forwarding mail or deleting a message. This is called prompt injection. GigaMail hands your agent only the text you would see, flags the rest, and asks you before anything is sent or deleted.

Can the checker be wrong?

Yes, both ways. It can flag an honest mail, for example from a small company whose domain looks like a brand, a newsletter sent through a mailing service, or a forwarded message that fails the sender checks. And it can miss a scam: it does not open links, a site set up today is not yet on the public lists of bad sites, it spots look-alikes only of popular sites and brands, and it does not read text inside images. Treat its result as a list of things to look at, and when in doubt contact the sender another way.

Where do the lists of bad and popular sites come from?

From public sources, rebuilt every day: Phishing.Database (MIT), the phishing filter by curben built from OpenPhish (CC BY-SA 4.0), URLhaus by abuse.ch (CC0), and the Tranco ranking of popular sites. The page downloads them from GitHub as hashes split in small pieces and fetches only the pieces it needs, so the sites named in your mail are never sent anywhere.

Is my email uploaded anywhere?

No. The check runs in your browser. The message is never sent to GigaMail or anyone else, and remote images in it are blocked, so the sender cannot tell you opened it here. The page only downloads, from GitHub, the lists it compares against.

How do I get the original message?

Easiest: save the mail as a file and open it here. In Gmail, the three dots menu, Download message; in Outlook, drag the mail to your desktop or use Save as. To copy it instead: Gmail, the three dots menu, Show original, Copy to clipboard; Outlook on the web, the three dots menu, View, View message source; Apple Mail, View, Message, Raw Source. Pasting just the text works too, but text copied from the screen leaves out the hidden part and the sender checks.
