GigaMail vs MachineMail

Looking for a MachineMail alternative? Both give AI agents email, in two different ways. MachineMail is a hosted service that creates new inboxes for your agents and scores every incoming mail for trust. GigaMail is an open-source MCP server that connects your agent to the mailboxes you already have, keeps everything on your machine, and lets nothing be sent or deleted until you approve it.

The short answer

Choose MachineMail

if your agents need addresses of their own: to sign up for services, catch verification codes, or run at scale on a domain you point at it.

Choose GigaMail

if you want Claude, Codex, OpenClaw, Hermes or any MCP client to read, search and draft replies in your own Outlook, Gmail or IMAP mailbox, with you approving every send.

When each one fits

MachineMail is the better fit when:

  • Agents must sign up for services or wait for verification codes under an address of their own, including throwaway ones.
  • You run many agents and want inboxes created on demand on your domain.
  • You want incoming mail scored before your agent sees it, as a managed service.

GigaMail is the better fit when:

  • You want your agent on the email you already get: search, triage, replies drafted from your price lists and documents, appointments in your calendar.
  • You do not want your mail, credentials or index on someone else's server.
  • You want a human in the loop that the agent cannot bypass: it gets an inert request id, you approve with Hello or Touch ID.
  • You want something free and open source you can read, audit and modify.

FAQ

Is GigaMail an open-source MachineMail alternative?

For agents that work on your own mailboxes, yes: GigaMail is AGPL-3.0 and free. If each agent needs its own new address, or a throwaway inbox to sign up for a service, that is what MachineMail does and GigaMail does not.

Can my agent send or delete email without me?

No. Send, reply, move and delete return only an inert request id. They run after you approve with Windows Hello or Touch ID (a local PIN on Linux), with the exact arguments you saw. An agent with a shell can open that prompt but cannot pass it. MachineMail offers drafts you can review, but an agent that calls send sends.

How do the two defend against prompt injection?

Both strip hidden text before the agent reads a mail. MachineMail adds a trust level per message, from verified to quarantined, computed in its cloud. GigaMail treats every mail as untrusted data and puts the guard on the actions instead: nothing leaves without your approval, whatever the mail convinced the agent of.

Where is my email stored?

With GigaMail, on your machine: archive, search index, credentials, sender memory and configuration. GigaMail runs no service and receives nothing. The mail your agent reads goes to that agent's model provider, so choose your agent accordingly; the masker can hide tax codes, IBANs, emails and phone numbers first. MachineMail stores mail in its cloud in the United States.
